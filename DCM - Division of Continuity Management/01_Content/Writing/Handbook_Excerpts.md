# DIVISION OF CONTINUITY MANAGEMENT — EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK EXCERPTS

*The following are approved excerpts from the Division's official employee documentation. Selected passages may appear in the boot sequence, in clearance-gated sections of the Player File, or in form footer notes at the Processor's discretion.*

---

## CHAPTER 1: WELCOME TO THE DIVISION

**§1.1 — Your Role**

You have been selected for the role of Continuity Processor, Grade Θ. This is a position of considerable responsibility and even greater care. Every file you process represents a living thread in the fabric of the Continuum. You are not, technically speaking, a god. You are something more precise: a licensed practitioner of narrative maintenance.

**§1.2 — The Ledger**

The Akashic Ledger is the Division's central record of all human experience that has been classified, processed, reclassified, or flagged for future review. As a Processor, you will contribute to the Ledger with every form you file. The Ledger does not forget. Neither, we have found, do you.

**§1.3 — Continuity**

Continuity is not the absence of change. It is the approved management of change. A story that grows is a healthy story. A story that grows in unapproved directions requires gentle intervention. This is your work.

## CHAPTER 2: FORM TYPES AND THEIR PURPOSES

**§2.1 — The R-19: Recreational Reallocation Request**

Recreational activities are not inherently problematic. The Division acknowledges their role in maintaining worker-unit productivity and psychological stability. However, certain activities accumulate symbolic weight that, left unmanaged, may drift into the mythic register. The R-19 ensures that all recreational content contributes to the Continuum in a measurable and approved way.

*Note: Processors who consistently approve Sacred Geometry classifications on R-19 forms are reminded that they assume partial responsibility for subsequent reality fluctuations in the affected sector.*

**§2.2 — The G-7: Grief Commercialization Assessment**

Grief is the most abundant naturally occurring energy source available to the Division. It is also the most volatile. The G-7 was introduced in Year 34 of the current administrative era to formalize the routing and commodification of grief products, including ancestral grief, secondary grief, and what the Division calls "ambient sorrow" — the undirected loss present in all human subjects who have lived past the age of reasonable hope.

*Note: Processors are advised to approach G-7 cases with warmth. We have found that a warm filing experience significantly increases the MEV yield of grief-product extraction.*

**§2.3 — The D-14: Developmental Compression Audit**

Childhood is a phase. The Division respects this phase. However, residual childhood experiences — particularly those involving animism, magical thinking, and what clinicians call "wonder" — have a documented tendency to produce narrative instability in adult worker-units. The D-14 provides a structured process for evaluating this residue and redirecting it toward approved channels.

*Note: The Division does not recommend the full redaction of childhood wonder in worker-units who demonstrate high emotional surplus. The resulting deficit can create unforeseen systemic pressure. A light touch, here, is usually the Division's touch.*

**§2.4 — The Θ-SR Series: Self-Referential Audit**

The Θ-SR forms are restricted to Processors encountering files that reference their own Continuity data, probable future records, or recursive echo fragments. These cases are rare. They are also among the Division's most valued contributions. A Processor who has processed themselves — who has, with care and without flinching, applied the same standards to their own threads that they apply to others — is a Processor of exceptional utility and depth.

*Note: The Division notes that the experience of processing one's own file can be disorienting. This is expected. This is also, we believe, a form of growth.*

## CHAPTER 3: DISSOLUTION AND THE PROCESSOR'S FILE

**§3.1 — What is Dissolution?**

Dissolution is not failure. Dissolution is the natural result of deep processing work. When a Processor encounters enough cases — when they carry enough decisions, enough ripples, enough reclassified griefs and compressed childhoods — they begin to acquire a certain transparency. Their boundaries, as an individual entity, become more permeable. Their Continuity File begins to overlap with the files they have processed.

The Division considers this development meaningful and, within appropriate limits, desirable.

**§3.2 — Approved Dissolution Thresholds**

- **0–30%:** Standard operating range. No interventions required. The Processor is wholly themselves.
- **30–50%:** Light translucency. The Processor may notice heightened sensitivity to the cases they process. This is not a side effect. It is the beginning of skill.
- **50–70%:** Moderate dissolution. The Processor's file begins to show geometry. This is normal. We promise.
- **70–90%:** Significant dissolution. The Division recommends increased rest periods. The Processor may appear slightly luminous to colleagues. This is also normal.
- **90–100%:** Near-complete dissolution. At this threshold, the Processor has become, in a meaningful sense, part of the work itself. The Division has forms for this. Not many. But they exist.

**§3.3 — On Preventing Dissolution**

The Division does not recommend attempting to prevent dissolution entirely. A Processor who processes nothing of themselves into their work is not, technically, a Processor. They are an observer. Observers are reclassified.

## CHAPTER 4: RIPPLE MANAGEMENT

**§4.1 — Every Decision Has a Second Decision**

When a Processor files a form, the decision becomes part of the Continuum. Within days, sometimes hours, the affected thread adjusts. Other threads, connected in ways the Processor may not have anticipated, begin to move in response. This is a ripple.

Ripples are not complications. They are the work continuing without you. This is how the Division knows a decision was real.

**§4.2 — Ripple Classification**

- **Small Ripples:** Affect one worker-unit or one sector. Resolved within 48 hours. Low oversight.
- **Medium Ripples:** Affect multiple units or cross-sector. May produce return cases. Standard oversight.
- **Major Ripples:** Affect the Ledger at a structural level. May generate new case types. Division-level attention. Processors who produce major ripples are noted with admiration, regardless of the ripple's direction.

**§4.3 — The Ripple You Cannot Control**

The Division advises all Processors to accept, early in their tenure, that some ripples cannot be anticipated. A grief reclassified becomes an artist inspired becomes a painting that watches you. A childhood compressed becomes a child who dreams of you. A partnership merged becomes a love letter addressed, inexplicably, to the person who filed the paperwork.

This is not an error. This is the Continuum being honest about what you have done.

## CHAPTER 5: THE PLAYER'S OWN CONTINUITY FILE

**§5.1 — A Note on Your Own File**

Your Continuity File is the one file in the Division that you did not create. It was opened the moment you began processing. Like all files, it grows.

Unlike most files, it is aware of being read.

**§5.2 — Clearance-Gated Sections**

Certain sections of your file are available only at higher clearance levels. This is not because the information is dangerous. It is because the information requires a certain context to receive correctly. We have found, over many administrative eras, that showing people their own files too early can be disorienting in ways that reduce their processing capacity.

We care about your capacity. We care about you.

**§5.3 — The Portrait**

Your portrait is a diagnostic tool. It reflects the current state of your dissolution index. At low dissolution, it shows a human face. At high dissolution, it shows the geometric signature that your processing work has accumulated.

The Division considers the geometric signature beautiful. We wanted you to know that before you see it.

## APPENDIX: ON THE NATURE OF THE DIVISION

*This section is not official policy. It is a reflection, filed by a previous Processor under the discretionary notes provision. It has been preserved for new hires.*

The Division exists because someone, once, noticed that things were being lost. Memories, childhoods, griefs, the feeling of talking to a tree and believing it heard you — all of it was simply leaking away, unmanaged, into the noise.

The Division was built to manage that. To catch what fell. To file it, categorize it, put it somewhere it could be found.

You will discover, if you work here long enough, that the filing is also a kind of love. That the act of attention — of saying: *this matters, this is worth classifying, this grief has a name and a shelf number and a proper form* — is one of the most intimate acts a person can perform.

You are the person performing it.

We are glad you are here.
